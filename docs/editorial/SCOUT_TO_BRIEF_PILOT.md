# Editorial Lab: Scout-to-Brief Pilot 01

**Status:** ACTIVE DRAFT / EVIDENCE-BASED PILOT  
**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Scope:** One research paper to one proposed commissioned original essay  
**Authority:** Research and pilot artefact, subordinate to `docs/00-DECISIONS.md`

This pilot exercises DEC-008 (proactive commissioning) and DEC-009 (AI-assisted editorial operations). It is not a contributor agreement, author invitation, editorial policy, final manuscript, or production system.

## 1. Minimum repeatable workflow (no new services)

**Input:** A primary research paper or original evidence source with its DOI/URL and accessible full text. If only an abstract is accessible, explicitly mark full-text review blocked and do not promote unsupported claims.

1. **Source intake.** Confirm title, authors, first-publication date vs issue date, DOI, evidence access, study method and its time frame. Save a direct primary-source URL.
2. **AI-assisted extraction.** Produce a short evidence map separating documented facts, author interpretations and the editor's original hypotheses. Record section anchors and uncertainties. Treat model output as unverified until checked against the source.
3. **Editorial angle search.** Propose up to three distinct public questions, not merely paraphrases of the paper. Evaluate novelty, evidence, strongest counterargument and suitability for an external author.
4. **Editorial gate.** Human editor selects, rejects or requests revision of the angle. Do not automatically choose the first or most provocative angle.
5. **Commissioning brief.** State the question, reader payoff, expected argumentative contribution, illustrative structure, source requirements, risk boundaries and author autonomy. Flag currentness and legal/copyright checks.
6. **Outreach gate.** No invitation, publication promise or email is sent until explicitly approved by the human editor. Fees, length and deadlines remain negotiable.

**Output contract:** Source card + evidence map + candidate angles + editorial judgment + proposed commissioning brief + unresolved checks + explicit human-approval status.

**No AI tool may be treated as an independent citation.** All externally consequential claims must be rechecked against primary evidence. Avoid writing to, uploading to, or copying unpublished manuscripts into a third-party model without appropriate permission.

This is currently an AI-assisted *manual* workflow: research discovery and synthesis happen in the editorial conversation, while the GitHub document is the durable result. No scheduler, agent service, database, CRM, model API subscription or Ghost integration is created.

## 2. Source card: verified original research

- **Paper:** Djordje Sredanovic and Émilien Fargues, "Governing Access to Nationality Through Paperwork: The Discretionary Uses of Documentation for Naturalisation in Belgium, France and the United Kingdom."
- **Journal:** *Social Policy & Administration*, 59(4), 679-688 (2025 issue); online publication 3 November 2024.
- **Persistent identifier:** https://doi.org/10.1111/spol.13098
- **Publisher full text:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/spol.13098
- **Method:** Comparative qualitative and ethnographic research, including interviews and observations of officials; fieldwork conducted primarily 2016-2018 in the three countries (paper section 4).
- **Scope/currentness:** Study describes administrative practices during its fieldwork period; the authors note the UK Nationality Checking Services ended in 2019 and French naturalisation filing was digitised in 2023 (abstract and section 5). This is **not** evidence of unchanged procedures in October 2026.
- **Access:** Full publisher HTML reviewed for this pilot. Data underlying the study are not publicly available due to ethical/privacy restrictions (publisher Data Availability Statement).

### Evidence map

| Source-backed observation | Where to verify | Editorial interpretation / caution |
|---|---|---|
| The authors describe three ideal-typical logics: the "right documents" (Belgium), "authentic documents" (UK), and "convincing documents" (France). | Abstract; introduction; section 7 | These are analytical ideal types, not exclusive rules for every official or case. |
| Before formal merits assessment, document checks may function as a practical gate to application registration. | Sections 5-6 | Distinguish a preliminary advice/checking step from an actual, legally operative refusal. |
| In studied Belgian and French settings, some refusals at the counter were verbal or unregistered; the study discusses how this complicates official refusal statistics and challenge routes. | Section 5 | Do not invent national prevalence rates or claim this still occurs in the same way in 2026. |

| The research emphasises that documentary assessment can be discretionary even when forms and documents appear objective. | Introduction; section 7 | This motivates a wider question; the wider normative claim is for the commissioned author to investigate, not a finding established by this one study. |

## 4. Pilot commissioning brief (internal, not yet sent)

### Initial working headline (not approved for outreach)
**The Decision Before the Decision**

### Public question
When an application can be stopped, discouraged or informally refused before a formal decision is recorded, what happens to the practical meaning of an individual's right to be considered?

### Reader payoff
A reader who thinks of administrative fairness as a matter of formal eligibility should come away distinguishing **the right to qualify**, **the ability to get a case accepted into the formal process**, and **the ability to challenge a decision**.

### Why this paper is the seed, not the whole essay
The source paper supplies a historically situated comparative account of naturalisation paperwork. The proposed original essay would ask a broader and genuinely contested question about administrative visibility and legal accountability, while staying precise about what the research demonstrates and what remains speculation.

The authors must be free to reject or modify this framing. We commission an investigation of the question, not a prescribed conclusion.

### Proposed author approach
First consider inviting the paper's authors, **Djordje Sredanovic and Émilien Fargues**, jointly or individually as appropriate. Their willingness, current affiliations, contact route and interest are **not verified**. Do not contact anyone without explicit approval.

### Possible narrative/argument sequence

1. An *accurately sourced* example of a registration-stage documentary obstacle, not a fictional composite presented as fact.
2. Why registration and formal adjudication are different procedural moments.
3. The Belgium/France/UK comparison, with the three documentary logics clearly labelled as ideal types from historical fieldwork.
4. What an undocumented gatekeeping practice does to observability, challenge and statistics.
5. Strongest objection: early checks can save applicants time and prevent inadmissible or fraudulent applications; their value depends on design and safeguards.
6. What would count as a fair balance, and what evidence is missing to judge contemporary practice.

### Evidence and editorial guardrails

- Primary research above is the source for the historical case only.
- Before making contemporary legal claims, independently verify up-to-date official procedures in each jurisdiction and identify where the process changed.
- Do not infer a national refusal rate from qualitative fieldwork or missing administrative records.
- Distinguish statutory requirements, discretionary administration, informal advice, refusal to register and final negative decisions.
- Identify strongest counterevidence and institutional justification, including fraud prevention and administrative efficiency.
- Do not invent real applicants, quotes, observation scenes or statistics.
- Any reuse of copyrighted passages or graphics requires a separate rights review; ask for a new original essay.
- No fixed length, fee, deadline or publication date is approved.

### Draft approach to a future invitation (concept only)
The note should reference the authors' specific 2024/2025 paper, explain the proposed *new* intellectual question in two or three sentences, make clear that the magazine is a new independent publication, describe the intended editorial support without exaggerating current reach, and ask whether they would like to discuss an original essay. **Not prepared for sending; no outreach approved.**

## 5. Adversarial editorial review (2026-10-10)

**Outcome: REVISE, do not invite on the original brief.** An attractive title is not evidence of an original commission.

### What passed

- Original Wiley text, DOI, bibliographic date and qualitative research frame are confirmed, including the fieldwork period 2016-2018 (source sections 4-5).
- The paper reports Belgian verbal refusals at municipal registers and counter-stage refusals in some studied French prefectures (section 5). These phenomena matter for visibility, appeal and administrative measurement.
- The essay's conceptual distinction between eligibility, registration/access, recorded decision and opportunity to challenge is potentially valuable to non-specialists.

### Material defects in the initial brief

1. **Originality failure.** The paper already discusses gatekeeping before registration and the difficulty of observing unrecorded refusals (section 5). "The Decision Before the Decision" as currently outlined would be a repackaging, not the new intellectual contribution DEC-008 requires.
2. **Risk of flattening three different systems.** Belgian municipal document checks were compulsory; the historical British Nationality Checking Service was optional and mainly advisory, and formally ended by 2019; French counter-stage practice depended on procedure and prefecture. Do not imply all three countries had the same state denial mechanism (source section 5).
3. **Rights overstatement.** A right to submit, a statutory entitlement to nationality, discretionary naturalisation, an appealable final refusal, and a procedural opportunity to request review are distinct. Do not assert a uniform general legal right to a merits hearing or appeal in all three systems.
4. **Historical-to-current gap.** French online naturalisation submission expanded in 2023, and UK applications increasingly use digital submission and document upload. The ethnographic observations do not describe present-day digital gatekeeping.
5. **No evidence for the larger proposed conclusion.** The statement that digitisation hides refusals would be an untested hypothesis; a digital portal might instead improve records, reduce discretion or create clear application timestamps. An original essay must distinguish these competing possibilities.

### Replacement exploration, not a settled commission

**Working question:** *Does Digital Bureaucracy Make Rejection More Accountable, or Simply Less Visible?*

**Reason to try it:** This could connect the documented historical problem of unregistered gatekeeping with a genuinely new empirical and conceptual issue: whether digital intake changes the observability and contestability of administrative filtering.

**The answer is unknown.** The title must not presuppose that digitalisation worsens rights protection. A credible writer must be free to find the opposite, a mixed result or an indeterminate one.

**Primary contemporary context checked on 2026-10-10:**

- [French Interior Ministry: procedures for access to nationality](https://www.immigration.interieur.gouv.fr/devenir-francais/procedures-dacces-a-nationalite-francaise). The ministry describes online filing for naturalisation by decree, requests to complete a dossier, closure when additions are not provided, and a distinct paper-based declaration procedure. This page also contains older informational material; date/check any individual legal rule before publication.
- [UK Government: citizenship applications after settlement](https://www.gov.uk/apply-citizenship-indefinite-leave-to-remain/how-to-apply) and [UKVCAS](https://www.gov.uk/ukvcas). These describe online application and supporting-document upload/biometrics, but do not establish the frequency or reviewability of digital-stage refusals.

**Required new evidence before an original argumentative commission:**

- Identify *one bounded 2026 administrative journey*, preferably French naturalisation by decree, rather than promising a fully up-to-date three-country comparative study.
- Describe what event legally/procedurally counts as starting an application, how requests for missing documents are recorded, and what happens if the portal or officer declines a filing.
- Find independently checkable official rules or service material on receipts, status histories, notice and challenge routes. Where rules and practice differ, seek newly documented observations from a qualified researcher, lawyer, civil-society organisation or other reliable source. Do not fabricate a representative case.
- Check the strongest favourable case for digital systems: date-stamped records, audit trails, standardized criteria, less informal discretion and accessible redress.
- Avoid claiming empirical prevalence, systemic illegality or comparative legal equivalence without supporting data.
- If credible contemporary evidence cannot be gathered without substantial new research, **do not commission a sweeping 2026 claim**. A narrower, clearly historical essay is possible, but its original contribution would still need to be independently identified.

**Proposed reader payoff:** The reader learns to ask *which step counts as an application, what is recorded at each step, and what can be challenged*. The historical study motivates those questions; fresh evidence must determine any modern answer.

**Author fit:** Original researchers might be appropriate if they have continued work on digitized nationality administration; verify that first. An administrative-law specialist with fresh case material could be a better fit. Do not presume availability, compensation, original reporting capacity or willingness.

**Gate:** HOLD FOR SOURCE/ORIGINALITY VALIDATION. Human editor must approve any future contact. No outreach has occurred.
